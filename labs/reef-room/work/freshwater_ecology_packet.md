# RAQ-R2 Freshwater Ecology and No-Leak Research Packet

## Original question anchor

What evidence-backed freshwater rules should a future hyperrealistic interactive aquarium simulation use for tank setup, chemistry, cycling, maturation, ugly phases, evaporation and top-off, filtration, organisms, welfare, compatibility, and common failures, while preventing marine chemistry, organisms, and equipment assumptions from entering freshwater mode?

## Bottom line

Freshwater mode cannot be one generic temperature, pH, hardness, stocking formula, or maintenance schedule. It needs a species and biotope profile layered on a shared physical model. The shared physical model includes water volume, heat, gas exchange, oxygen demand, nitrogen input, nitrification, alkalinity consumption, evaporation, conservative-solute concentration, filtration, waste export, and equipment failure. Species profiles then constrain temperature, pH, general hardness, alkalinity or carbonate hardness, current, swimming space, social group, substrate, cover, diet, and compatibility.

The safe setup loop is source-water testing and treatment, biotope and adult-stock planning, equipment and material installation, fishless biofilter cycling, measured proof that ammonia and nitrite are being processed, gradual stocking, then continued maturation. A visual "ugly phase" can occur as biofilm, algae, diatoms, and sometimes cyanobacteria establish, but it is neither universal nor proof that a tank is biologically safe. In a 2025 study of three home freshwater aquaria, ammonia and nitrite became undetectable by week 3 in two tanks and week 8 in the third, and biofilter community diversity changed across the 12-week observation period. This supports state-based progression, not a fixed calendar unlock ([McKnight et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41403708/)).

Evaporation removes water while leaving most dissolved material behind. Freshwater top-off therefore restores water volume with freshwater, never marine salt mix. Top-off does not export nitrate, dissolved organic matter, minerals, or other accumulated solutes, so it cannot replace water changes. UF/IFAS specifically warns that hardness, organics, and conductivity can rise in systems that receive top-off without routine water exchange ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)).

## Evidence classification used in this packet

- **Evidence-backed fact:** directly supported physical, chemical, biological, or welfare claim.
- **Conventional husbandry:** practice recommended by a veterinary, welfare, extension, or professional aquarium source, but still conditioned on species and system.
- **Design inference:** simulation rule logically derived from cited evidence. It is not itself a published husbandry standard.
- **Tunable gameplay:** a difficulty, pacing, visibility, or economic choice that must stay inside welfare and chemistry hard gates.
- **Ethical constraint:** a hard gate that prevents avoidable exposure to toxic water, incompatible conditions, predation, aggression, or inadequate space.

## 1. Freshwater setup and tank-size reasoning

### 1.1 Size is a constraint system, not gallons per fish

**Evidence-backed fact:** Larger water volumes generally change temperature and chemistry more slowly, and water surface area affects passive oxygen exchange. RSPCA advises that adult size, fish number, filtration, water source, temperature, and surface area all matter, and that simple length-per-volume rules are only rough guidance ([RSPCA aquarium environment](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment)). UK fish-care standards likewise require volume for normal swimming and stocking based on size, age, health, feeding, behavior, current, and water quality rather than one universal density ([UK fish care code](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-and-accommodation-of-animals-in-science-2026-code-of-practice/code-of-practice-for-the-care-and-accommodation-of-animals-bred-supplied-or-used-for-scientific-purposes-accessible)).

**Design inference:** Determine minimum tank geometry and capacity from all of these fields:

1. adult length, body depth, turning radius, and growth rate;
2. swimming mode and required uninterrupted horizontal distance;
3. benthic, midwater, surface, cave, shell, wood, or plant use;
4. shoal, pair, harem, solitary, or territorial social structure;
5. predator-prey mouth-size and hunting relationships;
6. waste production, diet, feeding rate, and biofilter capacity;
7. dissolved-oxygen demand, temperature, surface agitation, and backup aeration;
8. required territories, sight breaks, refuges, and breeding sites;
9. displacement by substrate, rock, wood, equipment, and air gap;
10. escape and jumping risk.

Linear "centimeters of fish per liter" rules should never be a welfare pass. At most, use them as a soft warning after adult size, behavior, group size, oxygen, and filtration have already passed.

### 1.2 Examples prove that there is no universal freshwater tank size

These are professional-trade husbandry examples, not universal minima:

| Freshwater group | Example husbandry envelope | Why it matters to the model |
|---|---|---|
| Small livebearers | OATA suggests at least 45 L for a small group of small guppies, and at least 80 L for sailfin mollies ([OATA guppies and mollies](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-guppies-and-mollies/)). | Group size and adult morph change the space gate even within one care category. |
| Discus | OATA gives 50 L per adult as a planning guide and about 300 L for six adults, with floor space and shoaling behavior also required ([OATA discus](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-discus/)). | Volume alone is insufficient. Group behavior, footprint, warm water, and water-change demand co-determine suitability. |
| Malawi cichlids | OATA suggests at least 200 L for a reasonable community, plus complex rockwork, strong filtration, aeration, and aggression management ([OATA Malawi cichlids](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-african-malawi-cichlids/)). | Territorial topology and social stocking logic must be evaluated with chemistry and capacity. |
| Very large "tankbuster" fish | OATA states that exceptionally large systems, often over 500 L, are required and many off-the-shelf aquaria are inadequate for adults ([OATA tankbusters](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-very-large-fish-tankbusters/)). | Juvenile sale size must never bypass the adult-space hard gate. |
| Shrimp and snails | OATA suggests at least 10 L for small species groups and about 20 L for larger shrimp or snails, with more space if fish are present ([OATA freshwater shrimp and snails](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-invertebrates/how-to-look-after-freshwater-shrimp-and-snails/)). | Low bioload does not eliminate stability, predation, escape, or mineral requirements. |

### 1.3 Setup order

**Conventional husbandry:**

1. Lock a freshwater biotope or compatible species set before buying livestock.
2. Inspect source water for temperature, pH, GH, alkalinity or KH, ammonia, chlorine or chloramine, and relevant contaminants. Municipal, well, and surface water have different hazards ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)).
3. Select a tank that passes adult-size, social, territory, oxygen, bioload, substrate, and escape gates.
4. Install safe substrate, rock, wood, plants, mechanical filtration, biological media, heater or chiller where required, thermometer, lighting, and aeration. Fill before energizing equipment that depends on water for cooling ([Merck, Providing a Home for Fish](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/fish/providing-a-home-for-fish)).
5. Treat all municipal replacement water for both chlorine and chloramine when applicable. Chloramine does not rapidly dissipate on standing or boiling ([US EPA chloramine guidance](https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/www3/region9/water/chloramine.html)).
6. Run and verify equipment, then complete fishless cycling. Equipment operation for 24 to 48 hours can establish temperature and expose leaks or faults, but it does not prove biofilter maturity ([OATA planted aquarium guide](https://ornamentalfish.org/wp-content/uploads/How-to-set-up-and-maintain-a-planted-aquarium-Sept-2022.pdf)).
7. Add livestock only after the cycle has passed chemistry-based readiness checks, then increase bioload slowly and retest after every addition.

## 2. Fishless cycling, nitrogen, and maturation

### 2.1 Why fishless cycling is the welfare default

**Evidence-backed fact:** Fish excrete ammonia, and uneaten feed and decaying organic matter add more. Nitrification is aerobic. Ammonia is oxidized through nitrite to less-toxic nitrate, and the process consumes oxygen and alkalinity while producing acidity ([UF/IFAS Ammonia in Aquatic Systems](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA031/pdf)). Aquarium biofilters provide high-surface-area habitat for these microbial communities ([Merck aquatic life-support components](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/aquatic-systems/aquatic-life-support-system-components)).

**Ethical constraint:** Do not expose fish, shrimp, snails, or other animals to an intentional ammonia or nitrite spike. RSPCA strongly advises fishless cycling, with livestock added only after ammonia and nitrite have risen and returned to zero ([RSPCA aquarium environment](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment)).

### 2.2 State-based cycle

| Cycle state | Chemistry and ecology | Player-visible cues | Unlock condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `source_water_locked` | Source water is characterized and treated. Total chlorine is 0 mg/L before contact with livestock or biofilter seed ([Merck reference ranges](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/multimedia/table/normal-reference-ranges-for-routine-water-quality-analysis)). | Test results, treated-water confirmation. | Biotope-compatible source-water plan passes. |
| `hardware_wet_test` | Filter, circulation, temperature control, water level, and aeration run without livestock. | Flow, leaks, heater response, temperature trend. | Stable operation, but no livestock unlock. |
| `ammonia_oxidizer_growth` | An animal-free ammonia source feeds the biofilter. UF/IFAS describes 2 to 3 mg/L TAN as one recirculating-system cycling method, not a universal home protocol ([UF/IFAS FA101](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA101/pdf)). | TAN rises, then begins to fall; nitrite appears. | Repeated test trend shows ammonia processing. |
| `nitrite_oxidizer_growth` | Nitrite rises and is converted to nitrate. Oxygen and alkalinity remain available. | Nitrite peaks, nitrate accumulates. | Repeated trend shows nitrite processing. |
| `commissioned` | A representative ammonia input is processed without persistent ammonia or nitrite. | Ammonia and nitrite return to 0 on the selected tests. | A chemistry-based challenge passes. A date alone cannot unlock. |
| `early_stocking` | Initial livestock adds real feeding and excretion. Biofilter demand changes. | Small nitrate rise, behavior and feeding observations. | No ammonia or nitrite recurrence, welfare stable. |
| `maturing` | Biofilter and surface communities continue to change while plants, algae, grazers, and detrital pathways develop. | Biofilm, plant growth, some algae or diatoms, changing water clarity. | No binary end point. Stability is trend-based. |

At 25 to 27 C, UF/IFAS reports that biofilters can require about 3 to 8 weeks to establish enough nitrifiers, with longer times at cooler temperatures ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)). The 2025 home-aquarium study independently observed undetectable ammonia and nitrite by week 3 in two tanks and by week 8 in the third ([McKnight et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41403708/)). These are evidence envelopes, not countdown timers.

### 2.3 Biofilm and microbial maturation

**Evidence-backed fact:** Freshwater aquarium biofilters contain diverse microbial communities, not a two-species cartoon. Recent work found comammox *Nitrospira* in all 38 sampled freshwater biofilters and distinct freshwater and saltwater biofilter communities ([McKnight and Neufeld, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38899882/)). In the 2025 start-up study, biofilter richness, evenness, and diversity increased during early weeks and were more consistent during weeks 7 to 12, while commissioning time differed among tanks ([McKnight et al., 2025 full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12704419/)).

**Design inference:** Model at least three microbial functions rather than named commercial strains:

- ammonia oxidation capacity;
- nitrite oxidation capacity;
- heterotrophic decomposition and oxygen demand.

Allow community capacity to grow on filter media, substrate, plants, rock, wood, and other wetted surfaces, but keep the biofilter as the dominant controllable high-flow surface. Capacity should respond to ammonia supply, nitrite supply, oxygen, alkalinity, temperature, flow, media area, clogging, cleaning, drying, disinfectant exposure, and long power outages.

## 3. Freshwater chemistry model

### 3.1 Chemistry is a coupled system

**Evidence-backed fact:** pH and temperature change the fraction of TAN present as toxic un-ionized ammonia. Higher pH and temperature increase the un-ionized fraction; UF/IFAS notes that un-ionized ammonia is much more toxic than ammonium ([UF/IFAS FA031](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA031/pdf)). Nitrification consumes alkalinity and produces hydrogen ions, so low alkalinity can cause pH decline, reduce biofilter performance, and create an ammonia or nitrite spike ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)).

**Design inference:** Never score ammonia risk from TAN alone. Calculate or look up un-ionized ammonia from TAN, temperature, pH, and the explicit unit basis. Never score pH independently of alkalinity, carbon dioxide, biological activity, source water, and any active substrate or additives.

### 3.2 Variable, range, and confidence table

### 3.3 Unit rules

**Evidence-backed fact:** Test kits can report nitrogen compounds either as the ion or as nitrogen. Merck notes that NO2-N multiplied by 3.3 gives NO2, and NO3-N multiplied by 4.4 gives NO3 ([Merck environmental hazards](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/multimedia/table/common-environmental-hazards-for-fish)).

**Hard interface requirement for A0:** Every nitrogen variable and threshold must declare its basis in the identifier and UI, such as `TAN_mg_N_L`, `NH3_mg_N_L`, `NO2_mg_N_L`, `NO2_mg_L`, `NO3_mg_N_L`, or `NO3_mg_L`. Never compare values with different bases silently.

## 4. Water source, chlorine, and chloramine

Municipal water may contain free chlorine or chloramine; well water may have low oxygen, high carbon dioxide, iron, hydrogen sulfide, or supersaturated gas; surface water can introduce pathogens or contaminants ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)).

**Evidence-backed fact:** Chlorine and monochloramine must be removed or neutralized for aquarium use because they can harm fish through the gills and inhibit beneficial bacteria ([US EPA monochloramine aquarium guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-09/documents/can_chloraminated_or_chlorinated_water_be_used_for_dialysis_or_in_an_aquarium.pdf)). The EPA states that chloramine does not rapidly dissipate by standing and does not dissipate by boiling ([US EPA Region 9 chloramine](https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/www3/region9/water/chloramine.html)).

**Source conflict to preserve:** Some general aquarium instructions say standing tap water for 24 hours can handle chlorine. That must never be treated as a chloramine solution. The simulation should require a disinfectant-specific conditioner or validated carbon treatment, followed by a total-chlorine result of 0 mg/L.

**Design inference:** A source-water profile should store disinfectant type, ammonia in source water, GH, KH or alkalinity, pH after aeration, nitrate, temperature, and optional contaminant flags. A utility switching from chlorine to chloramine should invalidate any "stand overnight" routine and can cause an incident if the player fails to update treatment.

## 5. Substrate, plants, wood, and rock

### 5.1 Substrate is biological, behavioral, and chemical

**Evidence-backed fact:** Grain size must fit the animal. Some fishes sift fine material, while others can swallow or lodge gravel; substrate dust can clog filters and damage pumps ([Merck, Providing a Home for Fish](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/fish/providing-a-home-for-fish)).

**Design inference:** Classify substrates by:

- grain-size distribution and sharpness;
- compaction and detritus trapping;
- plant-root suitability;
- cation and nutrient inventory;
- initial leaching or clouding;
- carbonate dissolution and effect on GH and alkalinity;
- color and behavioral effect;
- animal ingestion, digging, burrowing, and spawning suitability.

An inert sand or gravel should have minimal intended chemistry. An active planted substrate can leach nutrients and cloud water initially. OATA explicitly notes that some planted substrates do this and that dense initial planting can reduce early algal establishment ([OATA planted aquarium guide](https://ornamentalfish.org/wp-content/uploads/How-to-set-up-and-maintain-a-planted-aquarium-Sept-2022.pdf)).

Calcium-carbonate-bearing sand or rock can be a deliberate hardwater mechanic, not a default freshwater rule. OATA recommends coral sand or similar buffering substrate for Malawi cichlid systems that are intentionally hard and alkaline ([OATA Malawi cichlids](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-african-malawi-cichlids/)). It must not be represented as marine "live sand," imported marine microbiota, or an appropriate substrate for softwater fish.

### 5.2 Plants

Submerged plants can obtain nutrients from both sediment and water, depending on availability ([UF/IFAS aquatic plants](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA163/pdf)). In aquaria, plants can use some fish waste as fertilizer, but they do not eliminate the need for filtration or water changes ([OATA planted aquarium guide](https://ornamentalfish.org/wp-content/uploads/How-to-set-up-and-maintain-a-planted-aquarium-Sept-2022.pdf)).

**Design inference:** Track rooted versus water-column feeding, light, carbon availability, nitrogen and phosphorus, temperature, species-specific hardness and pH tolerance, herbivory, uprooting, shading, pruning, and biomass export. Plant uptake moves nutrients into biomass; only trimming and removal export that stored material.

CO2 enrichment is optional and species-system dependent. OATA warns that excessive or poorly controlled CO2 can reduce oxygen margin, promote algae, and cause pH fluctuation ([OATA planted aquarium guide](https://ornamentalfish.org/wp-content/uploads/How-to-set-up-and-maintain-a-planted-aquarium-Sept-2022.pdf)).

### 5.3 Wood and rock

Rock and wood provide cover, sight breaks, feeding surfaces, spawning sites, and territories. Merck notes that decor can divide the aquarium and help territorial fishes, including freshwater cichlids ([Merck, Providing a Home for Fish](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/all-other-pets/fish/providing-a-home-for-fish)).

**Design inference:** Every hardscape item needs composition, stability, sharpness, leachate, buoyancy, decay, and contamination properties. Use only aquarium-safe items. Secure heavy rock before digging fish can undermine it. Model tannin and dissolved-organic-carbon release only for an identified material profile. Natural blackwater acidity and color arise partly from humic and fulvic organic matter, but that does not make every piece of wood a predictable pH controller ([Morris et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33970558/)).

## 6. Filtration, flow, and oxygenation

Merck divides filtration into biological, mechanical, and chemical functions. Mechanical filtration should precede biological media so particulates do not clog flow paths and create anoxic zones. Biological media need high surface area, oxygen, alkalinity, and flow. Activated carbon can remove some toxins and color, but chemical filtration is not required in every design ([Merck aquatic life-support components](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/aquatic-systems/aquatic-life-support-system-components)).

**Freshwater equipment model:**

| Function | Required state variables | Common upgrade value | Failure consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanical filtration | particle load, pore size, bypass, head loss, cleaning state | more capture area, easier maintenance, prefilter redundancy | reduced flow, trapped decay, biofilter anoxia |
| Biological filtration | wetted area, community capacity, flow, oxygen, alkalinity, temperature | more media area, better oxygenation, redundant circulation | ammonia and nitrite rise |
| Chemical media | compound-specific capacity, exhaustion, contact time | targeted contaminant or color removal | breakthrough or false confidence |
| Gas exchange | surface area, agitation, air flow, temperature, biological oxygen demand | aerator, spray bar, backup air pump | hypoxia, reduced nitrification |
| Temperature control | heater or chiller power, thermostat, thermal mass, room temperature | guard, independent controller, redundancy, alarm | thermal stress, altered oxygen and metabolism |
| Monitoring | test type, resolution, calibration, sampling schedule | alarms, logging, trend detection | delayed incident recognition |

**Evidence-backed fact:** Low dissolved oxygen can result from high stocking, inadequate flow or aeration, high organic load, and high feeding ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)). Merck describes surface piping, flared gills, and larger fish being affected first as hypoxia signs ([Merck environmental hazards](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/multimedia/table/common-environmental-hazards-for-fish)).

**Design inference:** Oxygen is not a decorative bubble count. Model oxygen transfer from water-air interface and agitation, then consume it through fish respiration, microbial decomposition, nitrification, plant respiration in darkness, and elevated temperature. Flow must also match species. A hillstream fish and a long-finned betta cannot share one universal pump setting.

## 7. Freshwater algae, cyanobacteria, and the "ugly phase"

### 7.1 What the evidence supports

Algae are natural primary producers. Excess biomass can create aesthetic problems and oxygen depletion. Freshwater cyanobacteria are photosynthetic bacteria, often called blue-green algae, and can form water-column scums or benthic mats ([US EPA overview](https://www.epa.gov/habs/learn-about-harmful-algae-cyanobacteria-and-cyanotoxins)).

The EPA identifies interacting cyanobacterial bloom drivers including light intensity and duration, nutrient availability, temperature, pH, flow, and water-column stability. Bloom decay consumes oxygen. Toxin-producing and non-toxin-producing strains can look alike, so toxicity cannot be determined visually ([US EPA cyanobacteria fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-08/documents/cyanobacteria_factsheet.pdf)).

### 7.2 What the simulation should do

**Design inference:** Treat the freshwater ugly phase as a probabilistic ecological succession, not a mandatory scripted week:

- pale bacterial haze or surface biofilm can occur when readily available organic carbon is high;
- brown diatom-like films can occur on new surfaces, but their timing and intensity depend on source water, substrate, light, grazing, and nutrients;
- green algae respond to usable light and nutrient supply relative to plant demand;
- cyanobacterial mats become more likely with favorable light, nutrients, warmth, low-flow zones, and stable surfaces;
- all groups compete, graze, detach, decay, and add oxygen demand.

Visual ugliness is neither a failure by itself nor a cycle-completion signal. Chemistry, oxygen, animal behavior, and trajectory decide whether intervention is required.

**Conventional husbandry:** First correct causes: remove excess food and detritus, verify ammonia and nitrate trends, shorten excessive light, correct direct sunlight, restore plant health, improve appropriate flow and oxygenation, and perform species-safe water changes. Manual removal exports biomass. Avoid a universal algaecide button. Sudden biomass death can worsen hypoxia, and copper-based treatments are particularly dangerous to invertebrates. Freshwater shrimp and snails are highly copper-sensitive ([OATA freshwater shrimp and snails](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-invertebrates/how-to-look-after-freshwater-shrimp-and-snails/)).

## 8. Freshwater evaporation and top-off

### 8.1 Physical rule

**Evidence-backed fact:** Evaporation removes water and leaves dissolved salts behind, increasing dissolved-solids concentration ([USGS, Lake Mead chemistry](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0295/report.pdf)). UF/IFAS warns that hardness, conductivity, and organics may increase when a recirculating tank receives top-off but not routine water changes ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)).

For a conservative solute over a short step:

```text
C_after_evaporation = C_before * V_before / V_after
```

**Deterministic example:** If 100 L falls to 95 L through evaporation and solute mass is unchanged, conservative-solute concentration rises by about 5.26 percent. Adding 5 L of solute-free freshwater restores the previous concentration. Adding mineralized tap water also adds new solute mass.

### 8.2 Freshwater top-off rule

**Design inference:**

- An ATO may replace evaporation with freshwater only. It never doses reef salt mix.
- Purified water can restore evaporated H2O without adding more minerals. Source water can be used only if its added ion load is intentionally accepted and disinfectant is fully treated.
- Track water volume, each conservative-solute mass, and the composition of the top-off reservoir.
- Trigger ATO from evaporative water-level loss, not from water deliberately removed during a water change or spill. Those losses carry solute and require different replacement logic.
- Overfilling with purified water dilutes GH, KH, and other solutes. A stuck-off ATO concentrates them. A contaminated or untreated reservoir can cause chlorine, chloramine, ammonia, or mineral incidents.
- A lid reduces evaporation but changes gas exchange, heat retention, and escape risk.

### 8.3 Top-off is not a water change

Merck describes old tank syndrome in systems where water is added to replace evaporation but little water is exchanged. Nitrification consumes bicarbonate, total alkalinity falls, pH can crash, and TAN can accumulate. Recovery must be gradual because raising pH can suddenly increase ammonia toxicity ([Merck aquarium-fish management](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/aquarium-fish/management-of-aquarium-fish)).

**Hard rule:** Top-off restores volume. Water change exports and replaces water plus its dissolved and suspended contents. They are separate actions and separate UI verbs.

## 9. Water changes and maintenance

There is no universal water-change percentage. OATA gives up to 25 percent weekly as a general freshwater convention, while noting that frequency and volume depend on species, tank size, and stocking ([OATA water testing](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-test-water-quality-in-your-freshwater-tank-aquarium/)). Its discus guidance recommends 50 percent weekly for that warm, high-care group ([OATA discus](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-discus/)). These differences defeat one universal schedule.

**Design inference:** Calculate routine water-change need from nitrate and other accumulation trends, alkalinity consumption, source-water match, animal sensitivity, feeding, plant uptake and export, filter maintenance, and desired biotope stability. Replacement water must be conditioned, temperature-matched, and chemistry-matched closely enough to avoid shock.

Do not sterilize the whole ecosystem during normal cleaning. Mechanical debris should be removed, but established biological media should remain wet and should not be rinsed in chlorinated or chloraminated tap water. OATA recommends gentle rinsing in removed tank water ([OATA guppies and mollies](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/caresheets-tropical-freshwater-fish/how-to-look-after-guppies-and-mollies/)).

**Common-name trap:** Some fishes sold with "freshwater" in the name require brackish conditions during part of life. OATA notes this for some freshwater pufferfish ([OATA freshwater pufferfish](https://ornamentalfish.org/what-we-do/advice-information/care-sheets/brackish-fish/how-to-look-after-freshwater-puffer-fish/)). Under the locked project rules, these must be excluded from strict freshwater mode or placed in a separately declared brackish namespace. They must never silently activate marine salinity mechanics inside an ordinary freshwater tank.

| Failure | Observable state | Evidence-based cause | Recovery logic | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New tank syndrome | detectable ammonia or nitrite, reduced feeding, lethargy, abnormal swimming or breathing | bioload or feeding exceeds immature biofilter capacity | Stop new stocking. ## 13. Causal gameplay rules supported by this packet

1. **Feeding creates load.** More consumed feed increases metabolic ammonia; uneaten feed adds decomposition, oxygen demand, and nutrient load ([UF/IFAS FA031](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA031/pdf)).
2. **Biofilter capacity adapts slowly.** Adding livestock or feed faster than microbial capacity grows causes ammonia or nitrite recurrence ([OATA planted aquarium guide](https://ornamentalfish.org/wp-content/uploads/How-to-set-up-and-maintain-a-planted-aquarium-Sept-2022.pdf)).
3. **Nitrification spends oxygen and alkalinity.** High feed or low oxygen can create a coupled TAN, nitrite, alkalinity, and pH failure ([UF/IFAS FA099](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA099/pdf)).
4. **Temperature changes several systems.** It changes animal metabolism, oxygen saturation, nitrifier speed, and the toxic NH3 fraction. It must never be a cosmetic setpoint ([UF/IFAS FA031](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA031/pdf)).
5. **Plants relocate nutrients.** Uptake can reduce dissolved nitrogen, but only pruning and biomass removal export it. Plants do not replace the filter or water changes ([OATA planted aquarium guide](https://ornamentalfish.org/wp-content/uploads/How-to-set-up-and-maintain-a-planted-aquarium-Sept-2022.pdf)).
6. **Top-off restores volume only.** Water changes export dissolved and suspended load; ATO does not ([Merck aquarium-fish management](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/aquarium-fish/management-of-aquarium-fish)).
7. **Visual maturity and chemical readiness differ.** Biofilm or algae can appear before safe nitrification, and a clear tank can still contain toxic ammonia, nitrite, or chlorine ([RSPCA aquarium environment](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment)).
8. **Equipment upgrades change causal capacity, not a generic score.** A larger biofilter adds colonizable area only if flow, oxygen, alkalinity, and maintenance support it. Better mechanical filtration can protect the biofilter but becomes harmful when clogged ([Merck life-support components](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/aquatic-systems/aquatic-life-support-system-components)).
9. **Welfare hard gates precede ownership.** A player cannot buy or place an animal that fails adult space, chemistry, social group, or predation checks, even if later upgrades are planned.

## 15. Limitations and open questions

- Freshwater encompasses extreme chemical and thermal diversity. No single pH, temperature, GH, KH, nitrate limit, photoperiod, current, or stocking formula is defensible for every species.
- Group care sheets cannot replace a species-level database containing adult size, life stage, source population, captive-bred adaptation, social structure, and reproductive needs.
- The timing and taxa of a home-aquarium "ugly phase" are weakly standardized. Use probabilistic succession driven by substrate, inoculum, source water, light, nutrients, grazing, plants, and maintenance.
- The EPA cyanobacteria sources establish drivers, appearance uncertainty, and oxygen or toxin risk, but not a universal aquarium eradication protocol. Do not invent one.
- Conductivity and TDS are useful trends but do not reveal ion identity. Two waters with equal TDS may have different GH, KH, sodium, chloride, and biological suitability.
- Brackish and naturally saline-lake species need a separate design decision. Under the current locked rules, they cannot silently enter strict freshwater mode.
- Chemical treatments, antibiotics, parasiticides, copper, salt baths, and euthanasia require veterinary and jurisdiction-specific control. They are outside this packet's routine gameplay recommendations.
- This packet does not define freshwater breeding systems in detail. It only identifies chemistry, group, cover, substrate, diet, predation, and life-stage changes as required inputs.
- Freshwater micro-invertebrate taxonomy and population dynamics need a dedicated organism packet. This packet treats them only as freshwater community members and biofilm grazers or decomposers.
