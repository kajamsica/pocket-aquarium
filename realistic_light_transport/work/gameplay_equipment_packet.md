# RAQ-R5 Gameplay and Equipment Research Packet

## Bottom Line

A realistic aquarium game should treat equipment as a set of causal tools, not as linear stat bonuses. A useful equipment choice changes at least one of six things: life-support capacity, control precision, maintenance burden, observability, safety redundancy, or failure behavior. Brand prestige, vague "reef ready" labels, and unsupported biological claims should not become game advantages.

The shortest complete play loop is: choose a freshwater or marine namespace, plan around adult animal needs, build and leak-test the system, prepare source water, establish circulation and filtration, complete a fishless nitrogen cycle, pass through a variable new-tank maturation or "ugly" phase, quarantine and introduce suitable livestock, feed and maintain the system, respond to growth and reproduction, and diagnose faults from visible cues plus measurements. Every hidden state needs player-readable evidence. Equipment automation can improve response time and consistency, but it must introduce calibration, consumable, configuration, and single-point-failure risks.

For a reef aquarium, evaporation removes water while dissolved salts remain. The baseline auto-top-off (ATO) must therefore add only unsalted purified freshwater to replace the evaporated water. Saltwater belongs in marine water changes, and supplements belong in an explicit dosing system. These are separate mechanics.

## Lane Scope and Research Method

- **Original question anchor:** provide a source-backed foundation for a future hyperrealistic interactive reef and freshwater aquarium game, including equipment, setup, maintenance, progression, faults, diagnosis, recovery, feeding, breeding, and fair causal feedback.
- **Lane question:** which real aquarium equipment differences should become meaningful player choices, and how should they connect to interaction, maintenance, incident, and progression loops?
- **Research mode:** planned refinement research. The evidence plan separated life-support hardware, monitoring and automation, water preparation, reproduction support, and welfare gates, then synthesized them into action-state-feedback loops.
- **Closure mode:** discriminating probe plus reasoning. A hardware distinction was retained only when an authoritative or direct technical source established a different capability, burden, or failure mode, and a player-readable state consequence could be derived.
- **Stop condition:** every required equipment class has at least one meaningful state or maintenance consequence, every required player loop has a causal basis or an explicit design-inference label, and every serious fault has a fair clue and recovery path.
- **Surprise rule applied:** claims that resolved only to manufacturer marketing, with no defensible capability or failure distinction, were not converted into mechanics.
- **Prices:** not researched. Purchase prices, resale values, rewards, and operating-cost multipliers remain tunable design values.

## Evidence Classification

| Code | Meaning | Use in this packet |
|---|---|---|
| `FACT` | Evidence-backed physical, biological, or engineering behavior | Suitable as a simulation rule at the source's stated scope. |
| `CONVENTION` | Common husbandry practice with variable implementation | Suitable as a default workflow, not a universal law. |
| `PRODUCT` | A capability or failure behavior documented for a named product | Evidence that the capability is technically real, not proof that every product in the class has it. |
| `INFERENCE` | Game-system conclusion derived from sourced causes | Must remain visibly labeled during downstream drafting. |
| `TUNABLE` | Economy, timing, probability, UI assistance, or balance value | Designers set and test it. It must not masquerade as biology. |
| `ETHICAL` | Welfare or biosecurity restriction | A hard gate where allowing the action would reward preventable harm. |

## Equipment Class Inventory

### Physical system, filtration, and water movement

| Equipment class | Namespace | Defensible capability and causal state | Maintenance burden and credible failure modes | Monitoring and player-readable feedback | Meaningful upgrade dimensions | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display tank, stand, lid, and access | Both | More water volume generally buffers temperature and chemistry change, while surface area supports gas exchange. The tank must also fit adult animal size, behavior, and habitat, not merely current juvenile length. The RSPCA notes that larger volumes are more stable and that small decorative vessels lack room for filtration and adequate gas exchange. [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment) | Leak or seam failure, unlevel or under-rated stand, inaccessible maintenance zones, jump risk, and excessive heat or reduced gas exchange under a poorly managed cover. | Waterline, cabinet deformation, condensation, surface agitation, leak sensor, temperature trend, and maintenance-access preview. | Volume, footprint and swimming length, surface-area-to-volume ratio, material and scratch resistance, access, cover configuration, overflow readiness, and stand load rating. | `FACT`, then `ETHICAL` stocking inference. |
| All-in-one rear chamber or external sump | Primarily marine, optional in freshwater | A sump receives overflow water, gives equipment dedicated chambers, and returns treated water. A documented reef sump routes water through an overflow, mechanical filter, constant-height skimmer chamber, bubble trap, and return chamber; evaporation appears as a falling return-chamber level. [Red Sea REEFER manual](https://g1.redseafish.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/5501-REEFER-Series-Manual_EN_FR_DE_JP_CH_-v19A-web.pdf) | Dirty filter socks can bypass, valves accumulate deposits, drains can become noisy or restricted, return level can fall, pipe seals can leak, and too much operating water can leave inadequate drain-down capacity during a power cut. | Overflow noise, emergency-drain flow, chamber water heights, bubbles in display, floor water, return-flow rate, and pump power draw. | Working volume, equipment space, removable sections, emergency drain, plumbing serviceability, refugium space, drain-down reserve, and leak detection. | `PRODUCT` establishes one real architecture; class-level consequences are `INFERENCE`. |
| Mechanical filter media, such as floss, socks, pads, sponges, and rollers | Both | Mechanical filtration traps particles so they can be physically removed. Solids and detritus retained in filters can become a microbial reservoir when not removed. [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment), [SRAC recirculating-system biosecurity](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2013/09/SRAC-Publication-No.-4708-Biosecurity-in-Aquaculture-Part-2-Recirculating-Aquaculture-Systems.pdf) | Loading raises resistance and reduces flow or causes bypass. | Biological filter media, live rock, sand surfaces, and dedicated biofilters | Both, with separate chemistry | Nitrifying microorganisms colonize wet surfaces and oxidize ammonia to nitrite and then nitrate. New biofilters lack an established population and commonly require weeks to cycle, with rate affected by feeding load, pH, dissolved oxygen, alkalinity, and temperature. [UF/IFAS RAS guidance](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA101/pdf), [SRAC biofilter guide](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2013/09/SRAC-Publication-No.-4502-How-to-Start-a-Biofilter.pdf) | A new or overloaded biofilter has insufficient capacity. Drying, oxygen loss, inappropriate cleaning, medication, abrupt load increase, or severe chemistry change can reduce performance. Detritus trapped in media creates a separate maintenance burden. | Ammonia and nitrite curves, nitrate production, dissolved oxygen, pH and alkalinity trend, respiration cues, and measured processing capacity after a controlled input. | Effective colonizable area, oxygenation, flow distribution, solids prefiltration, accessibility, and reserve capacity. "More media" should not provide magic instant cycling. | `FACT`; cycle pacing is partly `TUNABLE`. |
| Chemical media, such as activated carbon or targeted adsorbent | Both, but media purpose differs | Chemical filtration can adsorb dissolved molecules. Research and professional aquatic-system sources describe carbon filtration as molecular adsorption and include it with mechanical, biological, and UV treatment. [FELASA/AALAS aquatic biosecurity recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9334003/), [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf) | Media has finite working capacity, can foul, and must be replaced or regenerated according to its actual purpose. Fine particles can escape if turbulent flow or containment is poor. It must not be a generic cure-all or remove unspecified compounds by magic. | Water tint or odor where applicable, contaminant-specific tests where available, media age or throughput, pressure or flow, and visible fines. | Target compound, capacity, contact flow, containment, replacement burden, and interaction with treatments. | `FACT` for adsorption; capacity numbers are product-specific unless sourced. |
| Marine protein skimmer | Marine only in the baseline game | Foam fractionation concentrates fine solids and dissolved organic compounds at the air-water interface, exports the resulting skimmate, and also contributes aeration. Skimmer water depth and air-water production affect performance. [Automated marine invertebrate rack system](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7199361/), [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf) | Cup fills, neck and air intake foul, salt deposits restrict the venturi, pump stalls, and incorrect water depth can produce no foam or excessively wet overflow. [Closed-system coral spawning protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5743687/) | Foam head, skimmate color and volume, cup level, air draw or power, sump water depth, microbubbles, odor, and dissolved-organic trend proxy. | Air and water throughput, stable adjustment range, cup sensor or drain, controllability, serviceability, noise, footprint, and overflow shutoff. | `FACT`; maintenance interval cited above is protocol-specific, not universal. |
| Return pump | Sump systems in either namespace | Moves water from sump to display and sets water-management turnover against plumbing head. Return flow is not identical to in-display coral flow. A documented sump manual notes that higher return-pump flow raises the display water level unless the overflow is modified, establishing a real coupling between pump delivery and plumbing level. [Red Sea REEFER manual](https://g1.redseafish.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/5501-REEFER-Series-Manual_EN_FR_DE_JP_CH_-v19A-web.pdf) | Impeller fouling, obstruction, dry-running or low-water stress, excess head loss, air ingestion, stall, disconnection, and power loss. | Return-chamber level, actual flow, overflow noise, bubbles, pump power, temperature, vibration, and controller status. | Rated flow at actual head, efficiency and heat, speed control, run-dry protection, serviceability, soft start, feedback telemetry, and redundant return path. | `FACT` for hydraulic behavior; named protections are `PRODUCT`. |
| In-display circulation pumps and wavemakers | Reef-critical, optional by freshwater habitat | Strategic placement creates flow around rock and organisms. Corals differ in flow tolerance, and random or variable flow can avoid a single harsh laminar jet. [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf) | Fouled or blocked propeller, animal guard obstruction, misalignment, detachment, stall, excess local velocity, dead zones behind rock, and power loss. One pump manual documents noise, heat, wear, stall, obstruction, and shutdown feedback as real product behaviors. [EcoTech VorTech manual](https://support.ecotechmarine.com/hc/en-us/article_attachments/4402315836823) | Polyp motion, food-particle trajectories, detritus settlement, surface agitation, pump noise and vibration, power draw, stall alert, and a flow-map overlay. Polyp extension or retraction is a clue, never a single definitive diagnosis. | Flow field and coverage, guard safety, controllability, efficiency, noise, mounting, feedback telemetry, battery backup, and ease of cleaning. | `FACT`, `PRODUCT`, and visual-feedback `INFERENCE`. |
| Air pump, airstone, or dedicated aeration | Common freshwater; emergency or special use in marine | Surface agitation and aeration support gas exchange. During a power outage, oxygen and water quality can become urgent, while feeding adds oxygen demand and waste. [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment), [NC State power-outage guidance](https://cvm.ncsu.edu/news/dealing-with-aquariums-and-ponds-during-power-outages/) | Airline kink, clogged stone, diaphragm wear, siphon backflow, noise, and loss of power. Excess bubbles near optical sensors can create false readings in some ATO products. [Tunze Osmolator manual](https://www.tunze.com/fileadmin/gebrauchsanleitungen/x3155.8888.pdf) | Bubble rate, surface movement, dissolved oxygen, fish ventilation and position, check-valve state, and pump sound. | Air output under depth, battery capability, noise, manifold control, check valve, service parts, and dissolved-oxygen feedback. | `FACT`; product interaction is `PRODUCT`. |
| UV sterilizer | Optional in either namespace, especially quarantine or shared systems | UV can reduce susceptible free-floating microorganisms that pass through the reactor, but it does not treat organisms attached in tank biofilms. Effective dose depends on intensity and exposure time, and is reduced by poor clarity, excess flow, lamp aging, or fouled sleeves. [NCBI water-quality chapter](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500450/), [Fish-facility pathogen recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3270489/) | Old lamp, dirty quartz sleeve, incorrect flow, poor prefiltration, burned-out lamp, and false confidence that the display is sterile. | Lamp-hour counter, UV intensity, flow, sleeve-fouling indicator, turbidity, and pathogen trend. | Validated dose at flow, sensor and alarm, bypass, prefiltration, sleeve access, lamp life, and energy use. | `FACT`; never a universal disease cure. |

### Temperature, lighting, measurement, automation, and water preparation

| Equipment class | Namespace | Defensible capability and causal state | Maintenance burden and credible failure modes | Monitoring and player-readable feedback | Meaningful upgrade dimensions | Evidence class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thermostatic heater | Both when species and ambient conditions require it | Raises water temperature toward a setpoint. Aquarium heaters can be inaccurate, can stop heating, or can heat continuously, so actual water temperature must be independently observed. [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf), [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment) | Stuck on, stuck off, inaccurate thermostat, cracked housing, poor placement, local hot zone, exposure during a low-water event, and power failure. | Independent thermometer, rate-of-change alarm, heater power state versus actual warming, animal stress, and controller discrepancy. | Capacity against ambient heat loss, thermostat quality, guard, material, multiple smaller units for redundancy, external controller, and replaceability. | `FACT`; redundancy benefit is `INFERENCE`. |
| Chiller, fan, ventilation, and ambient-temperature control | Mostly reef and warm-room systems | Removes heat or increases evaporative cooling. | Aquarium light and photoperiod timer | Reef and planted freshwater; basic day-night lighting for fish | Light intensity, spectrum, distribution, and duration are distinct. | Manual test kits, refractometer, thermometer, and meters | Both, with separate test menus | Clear-looking water can still be unsafe. During cycling, ammonia, nitrite, pH, temperature, and alkalinity are useful observations, and reef systems add salinity and calcium-system parameters. Colorimetric tests have lower resolution and more user subjectivity than good probes, while every method needs correct scope and standards. [UF/IFAS RAS guidance](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA101/pdf), [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf), [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment) | Expired or depleted reagent, contaminated vial, wrong sample volume, color-reading error, uncalibrated refractometer, wrong seawater versus brine scale, dirty optics, and skipped testing. | Replicate result, standard check, uncertainty band, reagent condition, procedural prompts, and trend log. | Parameters covered, resolution and accuracy, speed, calibration support, data logging, automation, consumable burden, and resistance to user error. | `FACT`. |
| Continuous probes and automated analyzers | Both, with different parameter sets | Continuous sensors can expose trends and drive alarms, but some probes require calibration, are affected by fouling or interfering variables, and can fail or disconnect. The NOAA manual notes response and interference limitations for some calcium probes. A named controller manual documents abnormal readings and the need to handle probe failure in heater logic. [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf), [Neptune Apex reference manual](https://help.neptunesystems.com/downloads/docs/Comprehensive_Reference_Manual.pdf) | Drift, fouling, broken cable, dried probe, bad calibration solution, air bubbles, interference, delayed response, and false automation. | Plausibility limits, rate-of-change, agreement with manual test, calibration age, power and communications, raw trend, and confidence state. | Accuracy and resolution, parameters, sampling interval, calibration workflow, redundancy, diagnostics, offline behavior, reagent burden, and data retention. | `FACT` plus `PRODUCT`; automation rules are `INFERENCE`. |
| Aquarium controller, controllable outlets, alarms, and data log | Both | A controller can log sensors, schedule equipment, alert on thresholds, and place outputs in defined fallback states. [Neptune Apex reference manual](https://help.neptunesystems.com/downloads/docs/Comprehensive_Reference_Manual.pdf) | Misconfiguration, bad sensor driving a bad actuator, communications loss, power loss, stuck outlet, alarm fatigue, network dependency, and hidden automation. | Event log, clear rule trace, command versus measured response, per-device power, local alarm, remote alert, and explicit degraded or manual mode. | Number and type of inputs, local operation, rule transparency, fail-safe states, power monitoring, leak detection, alert paths, data history, and redundancy. | `PRODUCT` establishes feasibility; generic progression is `INFERENCE`. |
| ATO sensor, controller, pump, freshwater reservoir, and tubing | Reef-critical; optional stability aid in freshwater | An ATO senses falling water level and pumps reservoir water to replace evaporation. A documented system uses a primary level sensor, separate high-level safety sensor, run-time cutoff, and alarm. [Tunze Osmolator manual](https://www.tunze.com/fileadmin/gebrauchsanleitungen/x3155.8888.pdf) | Empty reservoir, dirty or mineral-coated sensor, snail or obstruction, bubbles and turbulence, kinked or detached hose, failed pump, siphon, misplaced sensor, stuck-on fill, and power loss. | RO/DI source-water system and storage | Reef baseline; optional for freshwater depending on source and species | RO forces water through a semipermeable membrane, producing treated permeate and a reject stream. It can reduce dissolved solids and many contaminants, but performance and waste vary. [US EPA point-of-use RO guidance](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/point-use-reverse-osmosis-systems) Reef guidance uses purified water for artificial seawater and top-off to reduce uncontrolled source-water inputs. [UF/IFAS marine aquarium project](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/4H433) | Sediment or carbon prefilter exhaustion, membrane fouling or damage, depleted DI resin, low pressure, incorrect flushing, storage contamination, slow production, and reject-water waste. | Feed and product conductivity or TDS, rejection trend, pressure, cartridge throughput, DI exhaustion, storage level, leak, and product-versus-reject volume. | Contaminant reduction verified for the source, output rate, waste ratio, monitoring, staged cartridges, serviceability, storage, leak shutoff, and energy or water efficiency. | `FACT`; aquarium suitability still depends on source-water testing and downstream remineralization or salt mixing. |
| Dosing pump, reservoir, and manual dosing tools | Reef calcium-system support; planted freshwater fertilizers or CO2 are separate systems | Peristaltic or metering pumps can add measured solutions over time. Dosing can maintain consumed constituents, but inappropriate additions can cause pH spikes, precipitation, or ionic imbalance. [Closed-system coral spawning protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5743687/), [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf) | Empty reservoir, kinked or leaking tube, loss of prime, worn tubing, calibration error, wrong chemical or channel, stuck-on pump, precipitated line, incompatible container, and dosing from a bad sensor. | Reservoir level, delivered-versus-commanded volume, pump current, line bubbles, chemistry rate-of-change, precipitation cloud, pH alarm, and independent test. | Number of channels, minimum reliable dose, calibration, anti-siphon design, reservoir sensing, maximum daily limit, interlocks, service parts, and audit log. | `FACT`, with hardware details `PRODUCT`; economy values `TUNABLE`. |
| Refugium with macroalgae and protected microfauna | Marine baseline option; freshwater plant refuges are a separate design | A protected shared-water compartment can house macroalgae and small invertebrates. [Closed-system coral spawning protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5743687/), [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf) | Light failure, overgrowth, detritus accumulation, flow obstruction, macroalgae die-off, escape or predation of microfauna, and nutrient export falling when harvest is neglected. | Algal mass and growth, nitrate and phosphate trend, detritus, flow, photoperiod, oxygen or pH day-night trend, and visible microfauna count. | Volume, protected habitat complexity, light control, harvest access, flow, detritus management, and reverse-photoperiod option. | `FACT`; exact nutrient-removal rates are system-specific. |
| Quarantine or hospital system | Both, strictly separate by namespace and treatment compatibility | Physical isolation reduces pathogen introduction and permits observation or treatment without exposing the display. [UF/IFAS RAS guidance](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA101/pdf) A basic coral quarantine requires an appropriately sized tank, heater, circulation, adequate light, and optional filtration. [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf) | Uncycled quarantine biofilter, shared nets or water, treatment adsorption by rock or media, inadequate observation, cross-contamination, wrong light or flow for coral, and no plan for ammonia control. | Intake record, behavior and feeding, lesions or parasites, ammonia and temperature, equipment separation, treatment concentration, and countdown based on protocol rather than a magic universal number. | Isolation quality, independent tools, observation clarity, stable biofilter, medication compatibility, UV option, and space for separate cohorts. | `FACT`, `ETHICAL`, with durations species and pathogen specific. |
| Backup power and emergency kit | Both | Power loss simultaneously affects circulation, aeration, filtration, temperature control, and monitoring. ### Freshwater-specific equipment and namespace rules

| Class or rule | Freshwater function | Separation from reef mode | Evidence and design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dechlorination or source-water conditioning | Neutralizes chlorine or chloramine in applicable municipal source water before it contacts fish or biofilter organisms. | Reef mode instead starts from purified water plus a marine salt mix. A marine salt mix is never a freshwater conditioner. | Chlorine and chloramine control is a required freshwater setup and water-change check when present in the source. [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment), [UF urban aquaculture overview](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA217/pdf) (`FACT`, `ETHICAL`). |
| Remineralization and hardness control | RO or distilled freshwater may need species-appropriate salts, minerals, and electrolytes restored. | Freshwater GH, KH, conductivity, and species requirements use a separate chemistry namespace. Do not reuse reef calcium, alkalinity, salinity, or marine-salt controls as if they were equivalent. | Fish-facility guidance notes that salts, minerals, and electrolytes must be added after RO or distillation when required. [Fish-facility pathogen recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3270489/) (`FACT`, then `ETHICAL` separation). |
| Sponge, hang-on-back, canister, internal, or undergravel filter | Provides mechanical and biological filtration with different flow, access, media, and maintenance patterns. Air-driven sponge filtration also supplies gentle circulation and is useful in breeding or quarantine contexts. | Protein skimming is not a baseline freshwater progression requirement. It remains a marine class in the game unless a separately researched specialist freshwater case is added. | Mechanical and biological functions are supported by [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment) and the range of filter devices in [SRAC recirculating-system biosecurity](https://extension.rwfm.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/8/2013/09/SRAC-Publication-No.-4708-Biosecurity-in-Aquaculture-Part-2-Recirculating-Aquaculture-Systems.pdf). Form-factor tradeoffs are `INFERENCE`. |
| Heater or cooling control by species | Tropical freshwater species often need thermostatic heating; cool-water species may not. | Reef temperature targets must not be copied into freshwater. Every species uses its own acceptable and preferred range. | [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment) (`FACT`, `ETHICAL`). |
| Air pump and surface agitation | Often a visible, inexpensive oxygenation and circulation option, particularly for sponge filters and emergency backup. | Reef systems may obtain substantial gas exchange from overflow, skimming, and circulation, so air pumps are not a mandatory identical slot. | [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment), [NC State power-outage guidance](https://cvm.ncsu.edu/news/dealing-with-aquariums-and-ponds-during-power-outages/) (`FACT`). |
| Planted-tank light, fertilizer, and optional CO2 system | Supports a freshwater planted submode with plant-specific light and nutrient states. | It is not a reskin of coral PAR and reef dosing. Any detailed CO2 livestock-safety mechanic needs separate source work before inclusion. | Retain as an `INFERENCE` placeholder only. Do not assign numeric CO2, light, or fertilizer targets from the reef model. |
| Freshwater ATO | Replaces evaporated H2O and limits concentration of dissolved constituents. Purified or otherwise source-appropriate top-off water prevents an unmodeled mineral dose. | It never adds marine salt. Scheduled freshwater water changes still use properly conditioned and temperature-matched water, with minerals appropriate to the species. | A named ATO is documented for both freshwater and saltwater evaporation replacement. [Tunze Osmolator manual](https://www.tunze.com/fileadmin/gebrauchsanleitungen/x3155.8888.pdf) (`PRODUCT`; chemistry separation is `INFERENCE` and `ETHICAL`). |

## Consumables and Recurring Resource Classes

| Consumable | Real purpose | Depletion or misuse state | Gameplay treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marine salt mix | Creates replacement seawater for initial fill and marine water changes. | Wrong amount or incomplete mixing changes salinity and chemistry. ## Evaporation, Salinity, and ATO Mechanics

### Required mass balance

The NOAA coral manual states that water evaporation raises salinity and that deionized water can be added slowly in an area of high flow to restore the target water level. It also identifies higher temperature, increased air circulation, and greater air-water surface area as drivers of faster evaporation. [NOAA coral aquarium manual](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/736/noaa_736_DS1.pdf)

For a closed reef tank over a short interval, model the dissolved salt mass as conserved during evaporation:

```text
salt_mass = salinity_0 * water_volume_0
salinity_after_evaporation = salt_mass / (water_volume_0 - evaporated_volume)
```

Example, as a deterministic illustration rather than a husbandry target: a 100 L system at 35 parts per thousand that loses 2 L rises to about 35.71 parts per thousand if no other water or solute moves. Adding exactly 2 L of unsalted freshwater returns it to 35 parts per thousand. The same 2 percent evaporation produces about a 2.04 percent concentration increase in any nonvolatile dissolved constituent that is otherwise conserved.

### Separate fluid actions

| Player action | Fluid added | State change | Required UI language |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reef top-off | Unsalted purified freshwater only | Restores H2O volume and reverses evaporation-driven concentration | "Replace evaporated water" |
| Reef water change | Premixed replacement seawater matched to the intended salinity and relevant chemistry | Exports water and dissolved or suspended material, then restores volume and salt | "Remove and replace seawater" |
| Reef dosing | A measured solution with named solutes | Changes the named chemical inventory and may also add a small water volume | "Dose alkalinity," "dose calcium," or another explicit constituent |
| Freshwater top-off | Purified or otherwise validated source-appropriate freshwater, without marine salt | Restores H2O and limits concentration of existing minerals | "Replace evaporated water" |
| Freshwater water change | Dechlorinated, temperature-matched, species-appropriate water with required minerals | Exports waste and restores water and mineral balance | "Remove and replace conditioned freshwater" |

### ATO control and failure logic

1. The primary level sensor detects a low return chamber or display waterline.
2. The controller starts a small freshwater pump.
3. The waterline rises, salinity trends back toward baseline in reef mode, and the primary sensor stops the pump.
4. A separate high-level sensor, maximum run time, reservoir limit, and leak sensor can independently stop or constrain an overfill. A real product implements a primary sensor, high-level safety sensor, ten-minute cutoff, and alarm, which proves these are defensible class distinctions. [Tunze Osmolator manual](https://www.tunze.com/fileadmin/gebrauchsanleitungen/x3155.8888.pdf)
5. The player must still inspect and clean sensors, check tubing, verify reservoir water, and compare waterline events against salinity. Automation reduces repetitive input, not responsibility.

Fair failure chains:

- **Reservoir empty or pump/tube failure:** waterline falls, return pump may draw air, reef salinity rises, ATO run time reaches its limit, and an underfill alarm appears.
- **Primary sensor fouled low:** pump runs too long, high-level sensor stops it, and the player sees an alarm before livestock harm.
- **Primary sensor stuck on or siphon:** waterline exceeds target, reef salinity falls, reservoir volume decreases unexpectedly, and a leak or high-level alarm appears.
- **Sensor in turbulent or bubbly water:** rapid short cycling or inconsistent readings appear in the event log. Relocating or cleaning the sensor restores a stable pattern.
- **Unsafe large reservoir:** it offers longer unattended runtime but increases the maximum freshwater overfill. This is a meaningful risk-versus-convenience choice, not a pure upgrade.

## Progression and Economy Requirements

### Capability-led progression

Progression should unlock new responsibility and control, not simply larger numbers.

1. **Foundation access from the start:** an adequate tank, appropriate filter, temperature control where required, water preparation, and essential tests cannot be withheld to create artificial difficulty. The player must be able to keep the first legal stock humanely.
2. **Convenience upgrades:** larger media capacity, easier cleaning, better access, automatic mechanical filtration, dosing convenience, and remote logs reduce repetitive labor but create consumable or configuration costs.
3. **Observability upgrades:** better test resolution, continuous probes, PAR mapping, flow meters, power monitoring, and leak detection reduce uncertainty and detection time. They do not directly improve biology.
4. **Safety upgrades:** independent high-level ATO sensor, emergency overflow, redundant heater control, backup aeration, battery runtime, local alarms, and bounded dosing reduce the consequence or duration of a failure.
5. **Capacity upgrades:** larger tank, more suitable footprint, greater gas exchange, higher verified biofilter capacity, and more distributed flow expand what can be kept, but adult welfare and compatibility still decide whether an organism is legal.
6. **Advanced habitat upgrades:** refugium, coral-grade lighting, controllable circulation, chiller, quarantine expansion, breeding and larval systems, or planted-freshwater controls unlock specific husbandry opportunities.

| Maintenance | Task type, access, consumables, service interval estimate based on current load | Changes player labor and recurring cost. |
| Failure modes | Stall, clog, drift, stuck on, stuck off, leak, exhausted media, communications loss | Creates non-dominant choices and supports diagnosis. |
| Operating effects | Electricity, heat added to room or water, water waste, noise, footprint | Creates real tradeoffs rather than a universal best item. |
| Namespace and compatibility | Marine, freshwater, both, planted, coral, quarantine, medication-safe | Prevents cross-mode misuse. |

### Economy variables that remain tunable

- Purchase and resale price, financing, depreciation, and used-equipment risk.
- Electricity, source water, wastewater, consumable, and service costs.
- Labor time compression and task frequency multipliers.
- Shop inventory, delivery time, warranty, and failure probability.
- Reward pacing for stable husbandry, responsible captive propagation, education, research, or exhibit goals.
- Insurance or flood consequence, provided it does not trivialize animal welfare.

Responsible progression rules:

- Do not pay the player more for crowding broodstock or producing offspring without grow-out capacity.
- Do not let a premium item waive cycling, quarantine, chemistry, adult habitat, or compatibility.
- Do not rank brands. Rank traceable capabilities and costs.
- Used equipment can be a legitimate budget choice with visible inspection, service, and remaining-life uncertainty.
- Maintenance automation should exchange labor for calibration and consumables, not delete the underlying process.

## Difficulty, Realism, and Fair Warning

### Presets

| Preset | Information and timing | Failure model | What stays mandatory |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guided | Explanatory overlays, predicted effect arrows, generous pause or slowdown, automatic unit conversion, recommended tests, clear task queue | Slow fault escalation, simplified uncertainty, and strong alerts | Freshwater versus marine separation, adult habitat, incompatibility, cycling, correct reef ATO water, and humane stocking. |
| Standard | Sample-based tests, normal trend views, contextual hints after a cue, realistic maintenance categories | Moderate drift, clogs, stalls, and configuration mistakes with fair warning | All ethical gates and core causal mechanics. |
| Advanced simulation | Measurement uncertainty, calibration and reagent state, head loss, local flow and PAR maps, day-night coupling, source-water variability, more detailed dosing and quarantine | Multiple simultaneous faults are possible, but every severe outcome still has precursor evidence or an inspectable log | Ethical gates remain. Difficulty never means permitting cruelty as a strategy. |
| Scientific sandbox | Explicit units, equations, data export, controllable ambient conditions, no forced economy, reproducible seeds | Player-configurable within a clearly non-husbandry sandbox | Species and chemistry labels remain scientifically honest. Sandbox experiments are not presented as real husbandry recommendations. |

### Independent toggles

- Time scale and pause behavior.
- Chemistry granularity.
- Test procedural detail and measurement uncertainty.
- Equipment wear and fault frequency.
- Automatic task reminders versus self-managed logs.
- Visual diagnostic assistance.
- Economy pressure.
- Breeding and larval complexity.
- Controller rule complexity.

Welfare, adult-space, cross-salinity, and obviously predatory incompatibility gates should not be optional difficulty toggles.

### Fair-warning contract

1. **Glance cue:** show a plausible visual, sound, or behavior change, such as overflow noise, bubbles, detritus settlement, surface gasping, altered polyp motion, film growth, pump vibration, or waterline movement.
2. **Instrument cue:** expose the relevant trend or alarm, such as temperature rate, salinity, flow, power, ATO duration, ammonia, or reservoir level.
3. **Confirmatory action:** let the player inspect, cross-test, or isolate a cause before committing to treatment.
4. **Causal response:** the correct action should change the expected state first, then the biological outcome at an appropriate delay.
5. **Recovery evidence:** require a repeat measurement and improving trend, not a single button press.
6. **Incident record:** keep commands, alarms, maintenance, additions, livestock transfers, and measurements in one timeline.
7. **No perfect hidden controller:** automation reports degraded sensing, communications loss, and fallback state. A false but precise number must be possible only when cross-check cues also exist.
8. **No arbitrary instant death:** except for an unmistakably extreme action, serious harm should have a warning interval scaled to the process and difficulty. Time compression must preserve opportunity to react.

## Ethical Purchase and Stocking Gates

The following are hard restrictions, not economy penalties:

- Freshwater organisms cannot be purchased into or transferred to a marine system, and marine organisms cannot enter freshwater.
- The target tank must satisfy adult volume, footprint, swimming, refuge, substrate, flow, temperature, social grouping, and habitat needs defined by the species data, not just current sale size.
- A known predator-prey or severe aggression pairing is blocked when the prey cannot reliably escape and coexistence is not defensible.
- A shark or other large, high-mobility animal is unavailable unless the adult enclosure and professional-level life-support requirements in the livestock data are met. A higher player level or expensive filter cannot waive spatial welfare.
- Animals cannot be used to endure ammonia and nitrite as a cycling tool. Fishless cycling is the normal pathway. [RSPCA aquarium guidance](https://www.rspca.org.uk/adviceandwelfare/pets/fish/environment)
- A system must show stable, suitable chemistry, temperature, filtration, and oxygenation before stocking.
- New animals require an appropriate quarantine pathway or an explicitly documented lower-risk source protocol selected by the downstream livestock and disease design. The game should never reward knowingly bypassing biosecurity.
- Coral purchase requires a verified light and flow zone, stable reef chemistry, and space for growth and aggression. Polyp appearance alone is not acceptance evidence.
- Breeding requires humane larval and grow-out capacity or a credible placement plan. Excess offspring are not disposable currency.
- Medications and chemicals must respect organism, invertebrate, biofilter, and display compatibility. Ambiguous disease is not treated by random additive selection.

## Resolution of the Lane Unknown

The real equipment differences that should become meaningful player choices are:

- delivered capacity at actual system conditions;
- spatial coverage and distribution, especially flow and PAR;
- fixed versus adjustable versus automated control;
- measurement accuracy, resolution, scope, and logging;
- maintenance access, consumable burden, and serviceability;
- energy, heat, water waste, noise, and footprint;
- redundancy, independent safety cutoffs, and fallback states;
- failure detection, alarms, and diagnostic telemetry;
- compatibility with tank geometry, chemistry namespace, treatments, and other equipment.

Drop or mark unsupported any claimed benefit that cannot be connected to a measurable state, maintenance task, safety function, or failure mode. Examples include prestige tiers, vague "professional" or "reef ready" bonuses, unspecified detoxification, instant biological maturity, and brand-only reliability multipliers. If later product research supplies comparative test data, it can support a specific model; marketing copy alone cannot.

## Source-Quality Summary

- **Government and university extension:** NOAA and UF/IFAS supplied the core coral-system, cycling, monitoring, quarantine, feeding, and incident evidence. EPA supplied the RO mechanism and performance framework. These are the strongest class-level sources in this packet.
- **Peer-reviewed and professional aquatic-system literature:** the coral spawning mesocosm, automated marine invertebrate rack, aquatic biosecurity recommendations, and NCBI water-system chapter support real system architecture, refugium, dosing, breeding cues, UV limits, and maintenance burdens.
- **Animal-welfare guidance:** RSPCA material supports tank-size, filtration, fishless cycling, source-water treatment, temperature monitoring, and adult-welfare gates.
- **Manufacturer documentation:** Tunze, Red Sea, EcoTech, and Neptune sources are used only to establish product mechanics, alarms, redundancy, plumbing response, and documented failure behaviors. They are not treated as proof of biological superiority or whole-class performance.
- **Hobby convention:** the "ugly phase" is retained as a useful player-facing convention, but no fixed week-by-week succession or guaranteed organism order is asserted. Its component cues and causal variables are grounded separately.
- **No price evidence:** no current price or market ranking was collected, so all economy values remain tunable.

## Limitations

- This packet does not set species-specific tank volumes, compatible communities, predator-prey rules, reef chemistry targets, freshwater hardness targets, temperature ranges, or coral PAR ranges. Those must be merged from the dedicated marine, freshwater, engineering, and livestock packets.
- Product manuals prove that particular capabilities and failures exist. They do not establish that every device in a class performs equally, or that a named product should appear in the game.
- Home-aquarium maintenance frequency varies with load, system design, ambient conditions, and equipment. Protocol-specific daily or weekly examples must not become universal timers.
- The mass-balance equation assumes no simultaneous salt, water, or solute transfer other than the stated event. Real water changes, skimming, salt creep, wet export, dosing, leaks, and sampling alter inventories and should be modeled separately if the selected realism level requires them.
- "Ugly phase" timing and sequence are variable. A deterministic sequence would be misleading.
- Detailed planted-freshwater CO2 mechanics, medication pharmacology, electrical safety, plumbing structural calculations, and disease diagnosis require separate specialist research before numeric implementation.
- Polyp extension, fish appetite, color, water clarity, algae, and odor are useful clues but not unique diagnoses. The game should pair them with tests and trends.
